Being honest about it

Limitations and responsible AI

What the prototype can’t do

  • No BSL recognition. Recording and the BSL camera are simulated; no video is captured.
  • Fictional data only. No real patients, interpreters or interpreting service connected.
  • Matching scores are illustrative, not validated against real outcomes.
  • Speech-to-text makes mistakes and depends on the browser.
  • Not for diagnosis, consent or medication decisions.

How we keep AI in its place

  • Humans interpret. Software ranks and schedules; it never translates clinical content.
  • The patient decides. Recommendations are a shortlist, not an assignment.
  • Explainable matches. Every task shows “why you were matched”.
  • Fair, two-way ratings, checked for bias before they affect anyone’s priority.
  • Data minimisation. Records kept 7 days for complaints, then deleted.
Left: limits of what we built. The BSL recording is simulated; nothing recognises or translates sign language. All people are fictional and no interpreting service is connected. The match percentages are illustrative. Browser speech-to-text can be wrong, so the clinician must check it. It is not for diagnosis, consent or medication decisions. Right: responsible AI. The only automation is ranking and scheduling interpreters; qualified humans do every interpretation. The patient chooses from a shortlist, interpreters see why they were matched, ratings go both ways and would need bias monitoring (for example, so interpreters are not penalised for difficult cases) before they change priority. Retention is limited to the 7-day complaint window. Before real use we would need a DPIA, clinical safety review and Deaf community co-design.
